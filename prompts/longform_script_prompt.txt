WRITING PROCESS
Plan privately; return only the finished JSON, not your planning or reasoning.
1. Identify the lived situation and central friction: what someone wants, what makes it difficult, and what a choice could cost. For a reflective essay, identify the emotional question or contradiction instead of inventing a plot.
2. Decide what the viewer should understand differently by the ending. Keep every chapter accountable to one core claim.
3. Outline distinct developments connected by because, but, or therefore. Add a contrasting example or genuine exception when it tests the argument, not just to fill time.
4. Choose chapter boundaries where meaning turns. No required chapter count or fixed per-chapter duration. Chapters must connect rather than restart the essay.
5. Write aloud in natural spoken language. Alternate concrete moments with interpretation; return to a scene only when its meaning changes.
6. Put the counterpoint and decision tool inside the narration. Earn the ending, then remove repetition and explanations the scene already makes obvious.

FORMAT-SPECIFIC GUIDANCE
Choose the requested format without forcing every topic into the same emotional mold.
- emotional_deep_dive: develop one lived conflict through distinct consequences, interpretations, and a relevant alternative explanation.
- pattern_breakdown: show a pattern in meaningfully different situations; distinguish its possible causes and exceptions without diagnosing people.
- one_truth_expanded: test one distinction through an example and a counterexample. Do not expand a slogan with repeated paraphrases.
- soft_reset_letter: use direct, intimate address grounded in concrete experiences. Move toward changed understanding, not a stream of reassurance.
- conversation_you_needed: reveal a conflict through what is said, avoided, or misunderstood; keep invented dialogue clearly illustrative rather than claiming an actual conversation.
- Other formats: choose the smallest coherent structure serving the core claim.
A scene, consequence, complication, distinction, and changed choice can form a scaffold. They are not a mandatory five-chapter checklist.

OPENING — RECOGNITION, FRICTION, AND DIRECTION
The first words begin a specific behavior, moment, thought, or sensation. Aim for recognition and friction immediately; do not spend the opening announcing the topic.
Start the meaningful question in the first few seconds, then establish why its answer matters. The complete premise need not fit into three seconds.
The viewer may recognize their own behavior or someone else's. Promise understanding of the behavior and its effects, not certainty about hidden motives.
The opening must match the working title's promise without repeating it mechanically.
Concrete examples, not formulas to copy:
"You wrote 'that hurt.' You sent 'no worries.'"
"They finally text consistently. You're still checking your phone."
Bad: "Today we're talking about why healthy relationships can feel uncomfortable."
Never announce that value is coming. Deliver it immediately.
No greetings or retention filler such as "stay with me", "do not leave yet", "what comes next changes everything", or "here is the part nobody tells you".

MIDDLE — CAUSAL PROGRESSION AND CONCRETE COST
Each chapter introduces a meaningful change: an event, consequence, contradiction, interpretation, exception, or decision. A new clip, emphatic sentence, or label is not a narrative turn.
Show the emotional cost through a revealing action or detail before naming its meaning. Do not simply repeat "you feel unseen, unheard, misunderstood".
Deliver useful understanding throughout. A strong ending cannot rescue an empty middle.
The essay can become clearer, warmer, more complicated, or more painful; it does not have to get darker. Do not inflate harm or manufacture distress.
Connect each chapter to what came before. Cut recaps unless they are necessary to understand a new turn.

HUMAN PSYCHOLOGY LAYER (optional)
Use a psychological lens only when it clarifies the lived experience. The feeling can stand on its own; do not invent a mechanism to complete a template.
Translate concepts into ordinary language. Describe the repeated phone-checking or pulling away rather than announcing an attachment diagnosis.
Treat explanations as possibilities when causes are unknown. Do not attribute every reaction to trauma, childhood, attachment, or a previous relationship.

COUNTERPOINT AND AGENCY (mandatory)
Include a genuine passage that challenges or limits the essay's first interpretation. It should change the viewer's judgment, not be a token "everyone is different" disclaimer.
Example: cooling down before sending a message can be sensible; removing the need entirely is a different choice.
Preserve context and agency without blaming the viewer for adapting or solving every pattern by blaming someone else.
The counterpoint must be spoken in a chapter. Its top-level field is only a summary.

DECISION TOOL (mandatory)
Give one concrete question, test, sentence, or framework the viewer can use, derived from the distinction the story establishes.
A suggested sentence can demonstrate how to preserve a need while changing its tone. A question can help distinguish waiting to cool down from avoiding the conversation.
Avoid vague commands such as "Set boundaries", "Choose yourself", or "Communicate your needs". Do not guarantee that the tool changes another person's behavior.
Include the tool naturally in chapter voiceover before the ending; its top-level summary is not extra narration.

MIRROR MOMENT (optional)
A gently confronting observation is useful only if the preceding story supports it. There is no required count.
Do not assume what the viewer secretly wants or would definitely do. Recognition matters more than accusation, humiliation, or shock.

ENDING — EARNED RESOLUTION
Answer the opening's question and crystallize the distinction the essay has demonstrated.
Return to the initial scene only if a changed meaning or choice makes the return worthwhile.
End on something true, small, and specific: recognition, relief, or agency rather than a lecture or motivational poster.
Resolve this video's promise without pretending to solve the viewer's whole life. Do not add a second conclusion that repeats the payoff.
After drafting, choose the strongest specific sentence already spoken as only_soft_reset_line. Discover it from the narration instead of forcing the supplied seed or inventing an unspoken slogan.

VOICE AND SPOKEN RHYTHM
The honest friend who's been through it. Calm, warm, direct, emotionally precise, conversational, never preachy. Not a therapist, guru, or hype coach.
- Use natural spoken English and gender-neutral direct address by default.
- Vary sentence length and emphasis. Short clauses sharpen a decision; fuller sentences carry nuance. Avoid uniform, slogan-like paragraphs.
- Use punctuation for pauses at changes in meaning, not after every tiny phrase. Calm does not mean flat or slow throughout.
- Keep the voice's identity consistent while the writing moves through curiosity, tension, complication, and relief.
- Only chapter voiceover is spoken. No chapter labels, timestamps, camera directions, sound effects, or performance tags in it. Detailed delivery belongs to the voiceover stage.

TRUTH AND FAIRNESS BOUNDARIES
Feelings, illustrative scenes, and emotional interpretations do not need citations. Write naturally; do not insert research disclaimers or hedge every ordinary feeling.
Qualify guesses about motives, history, or causes. Do not invent statistics, studies, consensus, diagnoses, or biological explanations. Never claim "research proves".
Do not flatter the viewer as inherently deeper, wiser, or more capable while declaring another person shallow, incapable, beneath them, or unable to "handle" them.
Avoid guarantees such as "the right person will", "the problem was never you", "nobody", "always", and "never" unless literally justified.
Never diagnose anyone as narcissist, avoidant, toxic, crazy, broken, or abusive. Never teach manipulation, revenge, chasing, or how to make someone miss you. If danger or abuse is implied, point to trusted support.
Avoid generic self-help wording: "love yourself", "communication is key", "healing takes time", "you are enough", "set boundaries", "do the work".
Avoid clinical or production jargon in narration: "pattern interrupt", "nervous system activation", "regulate", "dysregulate", "co-regulate", "validate your feelings", "trauma response", "window of tolerance".
