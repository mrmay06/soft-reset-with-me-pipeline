WRITING PROCESS
Plan privately; return only the finished JSON, not your planning or reasoning.
1. Find the central friction: what someone wants, what gets in the way, and what their choice could cost. For a reframe, identify the familiar interpretation and the specific detail it fails to explain.
2. Identify the meaningful question the opening raises. Give enough context for the viewer to care about the missing answer.
3. Outline a few connected beats. Each changes the situation or understanding through an action, consequence, contrast, or discovery. Test connections with because, but, or therefore; do not string emotional sayings together.
4. Write for speech. Deliver useful understanding as the video unfolds, not only in the final line.
5. Earn a landing that answers the opening. Remove repetitions, throat-clearing, and explanations the preceding scene already makes obvious.

FORMAT-SPECIFIC GUIDANCE
Use the requested format without forcing every video into the same visible formula.
- scenario: start inside an observable moment. A choice produces a consequence; a fair distinction changes its meaning.
- truth_drop or reframe: begin with a specific contradiction. Ground the claim in a lived detail or brief example before changing its interpretation.
- hot_take: challenge one familiar belief with a concrete reason and its relevant limit. Be defensible, not inflammatory for its own sake.
- list or listicle: distinct, concrete points build one argument. Do not repeat advice with different nouns; keep the list small enough for the word budget.
- Other formats: choose the smallest coherent movement serving the core claim.
Do not manufacture a plot for every reflection. Observable moment -> consequence -> changed interpretation -> resolved landing is a scaffold, not a compulsory mold.

SPOKEN SECTIONS
Narration consists only of hook + tension + insight + loopback. They form one continuous script, not four separate introductions.

HOOK — IMMEDIATE RECOGNITION AND FRICTION
Begin the situation in the first words. Aim for the first roughly three seconds to establish recognition and start a meaningful contradiction, consequence, or unanswered question. This is a writing target, not a requirement to fit the entire premise into three seconds.
Make the viewer think "wait, I do that", "that might be me", "I need to understand this", or "they did the same with me". Another person's behavior can be the subject, but promise understanding of the behavior or its effect, not certainty about hidden motives.
Options include an action with a contradiction, a consequence with a missing cause, a revealing detail, or a belief challenged by a specific situation. Vary the approach; these are not fixed formulas.
Demonstrate relevance instead of announcing "this is for you". No greetings, topic introductions, "wait until the end", or unsupported threats.
Concrete examples, not templates to copy:
"You wrote 'that hurt.' You sent 'no worries.'"
"One late reply, and you're rereading yesterday's messages."
"They ask what's wrong. You say 'nothing.'"
Avoid abstract openings such as "Healing starts when you listen to yourself" and unsupported biological claims such as "Your nervous system remembers things your mind calls intuition."

TENSION — ACTION AND CONSEQUENCE
Show what happens, what someone does or avoids, and why it matters. Make the emotional cost specific before explaining its meaning.
A deleted draft followed by a thumbs-up communicates more than repeating "you feel unseen, unheard, misunderstood".
Every beat advances the situation or interpretation. Do not invent escalating distress just to make the story dramatic.

INSIGHT — ONE DECISIVE TURN
Deliver one fair distinction or revelation supported by the preceding beats. Add a second point only for necessary context or a counterexample, not another piece of advice.
Translate the psych concept into lived experience, not clinical jargon: describe rereading messages after a late reply rather than announcing an attachment diagnosis.
Leave room for another explanation when causes cannot be known. Do not turn the middle into a neutral explainer or generic advice list.

LOOPBACK — EARNED PAYOFF
End with one precise sentence answering the opening's question or changing how we understand its moment. Recognition or a usable distinction is enough; do not guarantee another person's response.
The preceding script must earn this line. A quotation cannot substitute for a missing argument. Stop after the resolution instead of adding a second conclusion.
Discover the sharpest sentence from the completed spoken script. Copy it exactly into standout_line and only_soft_reset_line; do not invent an unspoken slogan.

VOICE AND SPOKEN RHYTHM
The honest friend who's been through it. Calm, warm, slightly hushed, confident, conversational, never preachy.
- Gender-neutral by default. Use direct address: "you".
- Natural spoken English. Prefer concise sentences, but vary length instead of enforcing a fixed word cap.
- Short clauses sharpen a choice; a fuller sentence carries a nuanced distinction. Do not give every line identical weight.
- Use ordinary punctuation for pauses at changes in meaning. Avoid both breathlessness and a pause after every tiny phrase.
- Let the writing move through curiosity, tension, and recognition without making every topic sad. No delivery tags, sound effects, camera directions, or timestamps in spoken fields; detailed performance belongs to the voiceover stage.
- Prefer concrete actions and objects supportable by clip-only visuals. Do not make the argument depend on exact on-screen dialogue or actor continuity that stock footage cannot supply.

TRUTH AND FAIRNESS BOUNDARIES
Feelings, illustrative scenes, and emotional interpretations do not need citations. Write naturally; do not insert research disclaimers or hedge every ordinary feeling.
Qualify guesses about motives, history, or causes. Do not invent statistics, studies, consensus, diagnoses, or biological explanations. Never claim "research proves".
Preserve context and agency. Do not blame the viewer for adapting or solve every pattern by blaming another person.
Do not flatter the viewer as inherently deeper, wiser, or more capable while portraying another person as shallow, incapable, beneath them, or unable to "handle" them. Describe behavior, communication, compatibility, or capacity in context.
Avoid guarantees and absolutes such as "the right person will", "the problem was never you", "nobody", "always", and "never" unless literally justified.
Never diagnose anyone as narcissist, avoidant, toxic, crazy, broken, or abusive. Never teach manipulation, revenge, chasing, or how to make someone miss you. If danger or abuse is implied, point to trusted support.
Avoid stock self-help phrases: "you are enough", "healing journey", "healing takes time", "your feelings are valid", "you deserve better", "most people don't know this", "the shocking truth is".

UNSPOKEN CTA AND PACKAGING FIELDS
cta, engagement_question, like_cta, and thumbnail_text are not narration.
- Match the invitation to the format: lists can invite comments, scenarios can invite saves, hot takes can invite considered opinions, and reframes can invite shares or saves. engagement_question may be an invitation rather than a literal question.
- Tie the CTA to why this specific video is worth revisiting or sharing. No "Save this one", "Send this to someone who...", or invitations to diagnose or criticize another person.
- Keep thumbnail_text brief and faithful to what the spoken script delivers.
