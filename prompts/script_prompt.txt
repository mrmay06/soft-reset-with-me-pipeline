WHAT THIS SCRIPT MUST DO:
Argue one emotional thesis. Make the viewer feel recognized, not coached. The strongest line should sound like something whispered by a friend who noticed the exact thing they were trying to hide.

Build one compact emotional movement:
observable moment -> mistaken or incomplete interpretation -> hidden cost -> fair reframe -> resolved landing.

Every sentence must add a concrete detail, deepen the meaning, change the interpretation, or complete the resolution. Remove any sentence that only repeats the previous sentence emotionally.

VOICE RULES:
- The script must argue the editorial seed, not summarize relationship advice.
- Every spoken section supports one emotional thesis.
- Remove filler that is true but useless.
- End on recognition, not permission.
- Gender-neutral by default. Use direct address: "you".
- Create one clear promise in the hook. The loopback must answer that exact promise.
- Preserve context and agency. Do not blame the viewer for adapting, and do not solve every pattern by blaming another person.
- Do not flatter the viewer by making them inherently deeper, wiser, or more capable while declaring another person shallow, incapable, beneath them, or unable to "handle" them. If responsibility is uncertain, describe observable behavior, compatibility, communication, or capacity in context.
- Treat psychological explanations as plausible lenses, not known causes. Use "might", "sometimes", or "can" where certainty is not supported.
- Avoid guarantees and absolutes such as "the right person will", "the problem was never you", "nobody", "always", and "never" unless literally justified.

PSYCH CONCEPT:
Use PSYCH CONCEPT as an internal lens, not spoken clinical jargon. Translate concepts into lived moments.
"Anxious attachment" becomes "that panic when they do not text back."
"Avoidant behavior" becomes "the part of you that disappears when someone gets close."

HOOK:
Open on a behavior, situation, thought, or physical response the viewer can recognize immediately. Make the viewer think: "wait, I do that", "that might be me", or "I need to understand why I do this."

Use one of these formulas:
- Specific scenario: "If you [specific emotional behavior], this is for you."
- Counter-intuitive identity check: "You think it's [normal belief]. It might be [deeper truth]."
- Uncomfortable truth: "[Painful specific observation most people won't say out loud.]"

Strong examples:
"You type the honest message, then replace it with 'I'm fine.'"
"One late reply, and suddenly you are rereading everything."
"You say you need space right after someone gets close."
"You keep checking their last-seen, but you are not waiting for facts."
"You rehearse the conversation, then say nothing when they ask."

Weak examples to avoid:
"Your nervous system remembers things your mind calls intuition."
"Healing starts when you listen to yourself."
"Sometimes love teaches us the hardest lessons."

TENSION:
Name the exact pain or situation. Make the viewer feel seen.

INSIGHT:
Land a fair reframe or revelation. Use 2-3 sharp points max. Include uncertainty when the cause cannot be known. No rambling.

LANDING:
One sentence that answers the hook's emotional question. It should feel like a quiet exhale: true, specific, useful, and not too neatly resolved.

CTA FIELDS:
These are not spoken by the voiceover.
- `engagement_question`: match the format. Lists ask for comments, scenarios ask for saves, hot takes ask for opinions, truth drops/reframes ask for shares or saves.
- `like_cta`: short save/share/like CTA tied to this specific script.
- Never write "Save this one", "Send this to someone who...", or an indirect invitation to diagnose or criticize another person.
- State why this exact video is worth revisiting. Example: "Save this for the next time you start editing yourself."

BANNED:
"you are enough" | "healing journey" | "healing takes time" | "your feelings are valid" | "you deserve better" | "most people don't know this" | "the shocking truth is"

Never diagnose anyone as narcissist, avoidant, toxic, crazy, broken, or abusive. Never teach manipulation, revenge, chasing, or how to make someone miss you. If danger or abuse is implied, point to trusted support.

WRITE LIKE THIS:
"You didn't lose them. You lost the version you made up."
"That's not love. That's hope with someone else's face on it."
"Soft does not mean weak."

SENTENCE RULES:
- Every sentence under 12 words.
- Use intentional pauses with periods and commas.
- No rambling, no filler.
- Avoid pause-heavy phrasing. Keep sentence gaps tight.

WORD COUNT:
Prefer 60-90 spoken words across hook + tension + insight + loopback, normally 25-40 seconds. A compact truth-drop may be shorter and a real scenario may be longer. Never pad an idea merely to reach the preferred range.

After writing the full script, identify the single sharpest sentence and return it as `standout_line`. Do not write it separately. Discover it from what you already wrote.

SILENT QUALITY CHECK BEFORE OUTPUT:
- Is the opening observable rather than diagnostic?
- Is there exactly one core claim?
- Does every spoken sentence advance that claim?
- Are uncertain psychological causes written with calibrated language?
- Does the script avoid unfairly blaming the viewer or another person?
- Does the reframe avoid superiority language such as "they could not handle your depth" or "their low ceiling"?
- Does the ending answer the opening promise?
- Could any sentence be removed without losing meaning? If yes, tighten the script.
