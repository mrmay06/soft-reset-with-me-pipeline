# Research-Grounded Script Writing Design

Date: 2026-07-31

## Goal

Update the Soft Reset writing system so duration follows story capacity, attention is earned through meaningful narrative development, psychological explanations remain honest and non-diagnostic, and every opening promise receives a useful resolution.

This design covers Shorts and weekly long-form scripts, their configuration, validation and editorial review gates, tests, and operator documentation. It does not change publishing status, schedules, stock-footage providers, voice models, or thumbnail generation.

## Evidence and product interpretation

There is no defensible universal human attention-span number for video. Narrative research instead shows that engagement fluctuates with emotional content, suspense can focus attention on story-relevant information, and viewers naturally segment continuous stories into meaningful events that support comprehension and memory.

The product consequence is that the system must optimize narrative value density and event progression rather than insert arbitrary pattern interrupts. Channel analytics remain useful for later validation, but the existing sample is too small to establish an optimal duration and must not be treated as causal evidence.

## Approaches considered

### A. Compress weekly videos to 2:15-3:15

This reduces production burden and may improve completion percentage, but it leaves too little separation from Shorts and often cannot support recognition, mechanism, nuance, agency, and resolution. Rejected as the default. It remains a valid natural duration when topic research reveals limited story capacity.

### B. Default weekly videos to 4:30-6:30

This gives an emotional essay enough room for a recurring scene, multiple manifestations of the pattern, a plausible mechanism, an honest counterpoint, a decision tool, and a resolved ending without padding toward an advertising threshold. Selected.

### C. Target eight minutes for mid-roll eligibility

This improves theoretical monetization inventory only after the channel qualifies, but creates a strong incentive to repeat ideas and overuse stock footage. Rejected as a default. A 7:30-9:00 script is permitted only when the topic contains two distinct lived situations, a meaningful counterargument, a deeper mechanism, and enough original material to sustain it.

## Duration and capacity rules

### Shorts

- Preferred duration: 25-40 seconds.
- Preferred spoken length: 60-90 words.
- Hard spoken range: 45-110 words.
- A 15-24 second truth-drop or 40-55 second scenario is acceptable when the idea naturally requires it.
- The model must not pad a short to reach the preferred range.

A Short carries one compact emotional movement:

1. observable moment;
2. mistaken or incomplete interpretation;
3. hidden cost;
4. fair reframe;
5. resolved landing.

### Weekly long-form

- Default label: 4.5-6.5 minute emotional essay.
- Preferred spoken length: 700-950 words.
- Hard spoken range: 600-1,100 words.
- Longer 7.5-9 minute scripts are not generated by the default weekly path.

Before drafting, the model evaluates story capacity:

- one observation is a Short;
- one scene, one mechanism, and one reframe can support approximately 3-4 minutes;
- one recurring scene, multiple manifestations, a counterpoint, and a decision tool can support approximately 4.5-6.5 minutes.

If the assigned topic cannot sustain the configured range without repetition, the model returns `insufficient_story_capacity: true` with a concise reason. The pipeline treats this as a repairable research/script failure and requests a narrower or richer angle rather than rendering padded narration.

## Ethical attention design

The prompts may use self-recognition, honest curiosity, suspense, contrast, escalating consequences, narrative transportation, and resolution. They must not fabricate certainty, danger, urgency, secrets, diagnoses, or promised outcomes.

The writing system follows these rules:

- Open on an observable behavior, situation, thought, or physical response.
- Create one clear emotional question or promise.
- Deliver new value continuously; never announce that value is coming.
- Every section must add a detail, consequence, contradiction, interpretation, decision, or resolution.
- Psychological mechanisms are hypotheses, not diagnoses. Use calibrated language such as `might`, `sometimes`, and `can` where causes cannot be known.
- Preserve context and agency. Do not blame the viewer for adapting, and do not resolve every pattern by blaming another person or an allegedly wrong relationship.
- Avoid absolutes such as `always`, `never`, `nobody`, `the right person will`, and `the problem was never you` unless they are literally justified.
- The ending answers the opening promise and provides one usable distinction, question, or decision tool.

## Shorts prompt design

The Shorts prompt keeps its structured JSON contract but changes the generation logic:

- Hook: a specific recognizable behavior or contradiction, not a diagnostic label.
- Tension: the lived manifestation and hidden cost.
- Insight: a fair reframe that includes uncertainty where appropriate.
- Loopback: resolves the hook rather than becoming a generic affirmation.
- CTA fields remain unspoken and situation-specific. Generic `Save this one` and indirect call-out language such as `Send this to someone who...` are prohibited.

Before returning JSON, the model silently checks that the opening is observable, there is one core claim, every sentence advances it, causal claims are calibrated, neither side is unfairly blamed, the ending resolves the opening, and no meaningful sentence can be removed without loss.

## Long-form prompt design

The long-form script uses six functional movements rather than arbitrary retention lines:

1. **Recognition (0:00-0:25):** a specific recurring scene, emotional contradiction, and honest unanswered question.
2. **Pattern (0:25-1:20):** multiple manifestations without prematurely explaining them.
3. **Mechanism (1:20-2:40):** a plausible plain-language explanation.
4. **Cost (2:40-3:50):** the effect on self-trust, decisions, expectations, or relationships.
5. **Nuance and agency (3:50-5:00):** a counterinterpretation and a distinction the viewer controls.
6. **Decision and resolution (final 60-90 seconds):** one concrete test, return to the opening scene, and emotional closure.

These timings are proportional guides, not required timestamps. Each chapter must have a distinct narrative function.

The mandatory `stay with me` line and formulaic chapter re-hooks are removed. Instead, the script introduces a meaningful narrative turn roughly every 35-60 seconds. A turn must be a new event, consequence, contradiction, interpretation, or decision. Banned retention filler includes `stay with me`, `do not leave yet`, `what comes next changes everything`, and equivalent promises of delayed value.

The script includes one genuine counterpoint that challenges its first interpretation. It cannot attribute every reaction to trauma, attachment, childhood, or a previous relationship.

## Editorial review and validation

Deterministic validation continues to distinguish preferred targets from hard failures.

Short validation adds checks for:

- generic CTA language;
- unsupported absolutes and relationship guarantees;
- known retention-filler phrases;
- hard word-count bounds.

Long-form review adds checks for:

- repeated sections that do not advance the argument;
- announced-but-delayed value;
- unsupported psychological certainty;
- missing counterpoint or viewer agency;
- unresolved hook promise;
- insufficient story capacity;
- chapter functions that collapse into the same point.

A soft target miss may proceed if the story is coherent. Hard range violations, declared insufficient capacity, or failed argument review trigger one bounded repair. Exhaustion stops before rendering and preserves diagnostics.

## Visual implications

Voice and script carry the emotional narrative. Clip changes support changes in meaning rather than attempt to illustrate every noun.

- Shorts normally change clip or framing every 2.5-4.5 seconds.
- Long-form normally changes clip or framing every 4-8 seconds.
- A strong human expression may hold for 8-12 seconds.
- Larger subject, location, or composition changes align with narrative boundaries.

These are visual-direction preferences and must not cause repeated footage or override the existing uniqueness rules.

## Testing

Automated tests verify:

- configured preferred and hard word ranges;
- updated duration labels;
- required prompt language for capacity, calibrated psychology, counterpoint, resolution, and banned filler;
- validation behavior for soft target misses and hard failures;
- insufficient-capacity output stops before downstream rendering;
- existing private-test and paused-automation safety settings remain unchanged.

Prompt changes are evaluated with one mocked Short and one mocked long-form script before any provider-backed run. A subsequent private provider-backed test uses one Short and one long-form video; it does not enable public release.

## Documentation and rollout

README and the clip-only system specification are updated to state the preferred ranges, capacity-based exceptions, ethical retention principles, and the difference between a narrative turn and a visual cut.

Automation remains paused and uploads remain private throughout implementation and testing. Production activation requires a separate explicit decision.
